Processes¶

Organizational Processes¶

Organization Overview¶

The NTCIP effort is a joint effort of three Standards Development Organizations (SDOs): the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO), the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE), and the National Electrical Manufacturers Association (NEMA). The overall effort is managed by an NTCIP Coordinator and overseen by the Joint Committee on the NTCIP (JC), which consists of 6 representatives from each SDO. The JC establishes working groups (WGs) to develop standards and other documents per defined processes. The JC can also establish long-term advisory groups (AGs) and short-term ad-hoc groups (AHGs) to provide advice on specific topics.

Participation in the NTCIP effort is open to anyone who has a vested interest in Intelligent Transportation Systems, including infrastructure operators/owners, producers (e.g., device manufactures and central system providers), and general interest users (e.g., consultants, academics, and others). The JC and each of its groups (e.g., WG, AG, AHG) is led by a chair that is responsible for calling meetings and leading discussions. Other key participants include document editors and liaisons.

The responsibilities for each of these entities are described in the following clauses.

Assignment¶

SDO membership within the NTCIP is predicated upon joint agreement among all participating SDOs.

Responsibilities¶

Each of the three SDO members of the NTCIP effort is responsible for:

  1. Establishing the necessary Memorandum of Understandings among the SDOs
  2. Establishment of Joint Committees and scope
  3. Appointing members to the Joint Committees and coordinating with other SDOs to ensure balance
  4. Appointing the NTCIP Coordinator
  5. Coordination of technical work among SDOs and among Joint Committees
  6. Consideration of appeals

Termination¶

Rules for an SDO to terminate its participation within the NTCIP are defined in the joint agreement among participating SDOs.

Joint Committee on the NTCIP¶

Parameter Value
Effective Date: September 30, 2026
Last Reviewed/Updated: September 30, 2026

Establishment¶

The Joint Committee on the NTCIP (NTCIP JC) is established by the Memorandum of Understanding among the SDOs.

Membership¶

The NTCIP JC is composed of the following voting membership interest categories:

  • AASHTO (user)
  • ITE (general interest)
  • NEMA (producer)

Each SDO is represented by six (6) voting members, for a total of eighteen (18) voting members.

NTCIP JC meetings are typically open meetings and can be attended by any interested party. These parties are encouraged to register with the NTCIP Coordinator as observers or liaisons to participate in discussions and receive meeting announcements.

Responsibilities¶

The JC is responsible for:

  1. Approving the policies, processes and procedures of the NTCIP
  2. Approving new projects with appropriate details
  3. Approving the formation of new groups (e.g., WGs, AGs, AHGs) under the NTCIP JC
  4. Approving recommendations from established groups, as appropriate (e.g., approving a Recommended Standard, approving a major version update of an existing NTCIP publication, approving sending a document to industry ballot)

Quorum¶

Question

Based on discussions; for review.

The prior version indicated ⅓rd of the voting membership with one voting member present from each of the NTCIP SDOs. This was changed to ⅔rds and two members from each SDO to align WG discussions.

A quorum is required for any formal action by the NTCIP JC. Quorum is established by a ⅔rds majority of the current voting membership with at least two (2) members from each of the three (3) membership categories (AASHTO, ITE, and NEMA). Alternates count towards the establishment of a quorum.

Note

When a quorum is not present, the NTCIP JC can proceed with its meeting, but cannot take any formal action unless additional voting members arrive.

Example

If there are 18 voting members, then at least 12 voting members must be present.

If there are 17 voting members, then at least 12 voting members must be present.

If there are 16 voting members, then at least 11 voting members must be present.

Voting¶

Question

Based on discussions; for review.

In general, the NTCIP JC operates on a consensus basis (i.e., reasonable attempts are made to resolve all sustained objections). However, when needed to resolve conflicts and when required by adopted procedures, the NTCIP JC will undertake formal votes. Unless otherwise stated in detailed processes, decisions by the JC are made by simple majority. Detailed procedures for voting are defined in NTCIP JC voting procedures.

Types of Groups¶

Within the NTCIP effort, most work is performed within groups. Only the NTCIP JC may establish working groups. The NTCIP JC and its WGs may establish maintenance, advisory and ad-hoc groups as needed.

Question

Added to reflect discussions. Is this appropriate (especially maintenance groups)?

  1. Working Groups are formed for the task of developing one or more documents (e.g., standards, informational reports) to be published by the NTCIP.
  2. Maintenance Groups are formed for the task of quickly approving patches to published documents and triaging issues identified within existing publications.
  3. Advisory Groups are long-term groups formed to provide guidance and recommendations to its parent on issues that continually evolve (e.g., planning, coordination).
  4. Ad-hoc Groups are short-term groups formed to provide guidance and recommendations to its parent on specific issues that arise.

Working Groups are responsible for preparing NTCIP publications assigned to it by the NTCIP JC. A working group may approve minor version updates to IFR documents.

Maintenance Groups are responsible for performing triage on issues submitted on published NTCIP documents to which it has been assigned. They should recommend an approach to addressing the issue and, when deemed necessary, may directly approve patches to the publication per defined procedures. Patches must be limited to those that do not require the addition of new content.

Note

When timelines allow, it is preferable for a maintenance group to make a recommendation to its parent working group to ensure that the proposed solution is supported by the full working group.

Advisory and ad-hoc groups only provide advice on topics. The advice may be delivered in the form of an internal written report; however, all external publications of the NTCIP must be prepared by working groups.

Establishment of groups¶

Question

Can groups be established by the chair of the parent group or only through simple majority vote by the parent group? Or perhaps WG by majority vote and other groups by the chair?

Groups are established by a simple majority vote by the parent group (i.e., either the NTCIP JC or one of its working groups).

The NTCIP Coordinator is responsible for publicizing working groups to promote membership within the group whenever:

  • A working group is established,
  • Membership falls below defined thresholds, and
  • The working group is reactivated after being dormant for a period of time.

Membership in Working Groups¶

Question

Based on discussions; for review.

There are three categories of members within NTCIP WGs: Voting members, observing members, and liaison members.

Each working group must be composed of the following voting membership interest categories:

  • Users (public sector / user community)
  • Producers (private product developers for the standard, including either side of an interface as discussed)
  • General Interest (neither of the above for that working group; this generally includes consultants and academics)

Each interest category must be assigned the same number of voting positions within the working group; the number of voting positions within the working group must be determined by the NTCIP JC and must include at least two (2) voting positions for each interest category. Any working group that does not have at least two (2) voting positions filled for each interest category will be considered inactive until the limitation is resolved.

When expressing interest in a working group, the participant must assert their interest category. Any member of a working group may appeal the validity of an interest category assertion of another member; in this case, the appeal must be considered by the working group co-chairs, the NTCIP JC chair, the NTCIP Coordinator, and the SDOs to make a final determination of the participant's interest category.

NTCIP working group meetings must be open meetings that can be attended by any interested party. These parties are encouraged to register with the NTCIP Coordinator as observers or liaisons to participate in discussions and receive meeting announcements.

Membership in Maintenance Groups¶

Question

For review.

Each maintenance group must be composed of the following voting membership interest categories:

  • Users (public sector / user community)
  • Producers (private product developers for the standard, including either side of an interface as discussed)
  • General Interest (neither of the above for that working group; this generally includes consultants and academics)

Each interest category must be assigned the same number of voting positions within the maintenance group; the number of voting positions within the maintenance group and the individuals fulfilling those positions must be determined by the parent group (i.e., the NTCIP JC or a working group).

Maintenance group meetings can be called as soon as all maintenance group members are available without any formal public notice. Minutes or transcripts of the meetings must be made available to the public within 10 days of the meeting.

Membership in Advisory and Ad-hoc Groups¶

Question

Based on discussions; for review.

Each advisory and ad-hoc group must be composed of voting positions as determined by its parent group.

Note

While advisory and ad-hoc groups are often designed to have balanced membership, this is not a hard requirement because their scope is limited to the development of internal documents that are reviewed by a parent group that is already balanced.

Responsibilities¶

Each group is responsible for activities defined by its scope. When there is active work within the group, the group is responsible for reporting to its parent regarding its current schedule and progress made as well as reporting any issues that have arisen and making formal recommendations for any action that needs to be taken.

Quorum¶

A quorum is required for any formal action by a group. To establish a quorum, a group will have greater than 50% of the current voting membership in attendance.

Note

When a quorum is not present, the group can proceed with its meeting, but cannot take any formal action unless additional voting members arrive.

Voting¶

Each group generally operates by consensus. Specifically, issues about the text of a draft standard are discussed, as necessary. If there are no sustained objections to the revised text, the discussion can move to the next topic without any formal vote. This promotes speedy development; nonetheless, a member can use Robert's Rules to ask for a vote on the issue to document the level of consensus and/or to move the conversation forward, if they wish. While most discussions are by consensus, key decisions defined in the development process require formal votes to unambiguously document the level of consensus achieved prior to moving the document to the next stage. Detailed procedures for voting within an NTCIP group are defined in NTCIP WG voting procedures.

Terminating groups¶

At any time, the NTCIP JC can suspend or terminate a group by a simple majority vote.

JC Leadership¶

Parameter Value
Effective Date: September 30, 2026
Last Reviewed/Updated: September 30, 2026

Assignment¶

Discussion

This is a significant change from the draft MOU; for review.

Each of the three SDOs must be assigned one NTCIP Leadership role. The NTCIP Leadership roles consist of a Chair, a Senior Vice-Chair, and a Junior Vice-Chair, with positions rotating among the three SDO assignees every two years, resulting in a 6-year cycle.

Each SDO is responsible for assigning a person to fill its NTCIP Leadership role. The person appointed is not required to be a member of the SDO or to be employed by an organizational member of the SDO, nor is the person required to be a voting member of the NTCIP JC; it can be any person who the SDO deems to be qualified for the role (e.g., an academic can be assigned by AASHTO, ITE, or NEMA), with the exception that the person appointed must not be employed by the same organization as any other Leadership role assignee. It is the responsibility of the SDOs to ensure that each Leadership role is filled by a person from a different organization.

Conceptually, the Leadership Role should be viewed as a six-year term, with the first two years being the Junior Vice-Chair, the next two years being the Senior Vice-Chair, and the final two years being the Chair. This allows the Chair to gain experience and knowledge of the NTCIP effort before taking on the role of Chair. However, this is not a hard requirement and the SDOs are free to assign the Leadership role to any person they deem to be qualified for the role at any time. The SDOs may also negotiate changing the order to Leadership rotation to ensure the best interest of the NTCIP effort.

If an SDO has left their seat empty for more than 6 months at the time of rotation, their position will be assigned to the Junior Vice Chair. If this condition applies to multiple SDOs, the Junior Vice Chair will be assigned to the SDO with the longest consecutive period of vacancy.

Responsibilities¶

Each Leadership Role assignee is responsible for:

  1. Participating in meetings,
  2. Acting on behalf of the full NTCIP effort (not their employer or the SDO they represent),
  3. Guiding the NTCIP Coordinator,
  4. Ensuring projects are moving forwards,
  5. Calling JC meetings,
  6. Presiding over NTCIP JC meetings,
    • Ensuring that important topics are included in the agenda,
    • Pursuing goal of establishing consensus on important issues,
    • Ensuring that all concerns are properly considered by the committee in a balanced manner, and
    • Ensuring that all committee decisions are properly formulated and captured,
  7. Appointing chairs of newly established groups and filling vacancies in existing groups,
  8. Ensuring established processes are followed, and
  9. Ensuring the establishment of a long-term plan for the NTCIP effort, and
  10. Informing the NTCIP Coordinator of any changes to their contact or employment information.

In all cases, the Chair must take the lead in these activities. The Senior Vice-Chair must assist the Chair in these activities and act as the Chair in the absence of the Chair. The Junior Vice-Chair must assist the Senior Vice-Chair in these activities and act as the Senior Vice-Chair in the absence of the Senior Vice-Chair and the Chair in the absence of both the Senior Vice-Chair and the Chair.

Termination¶

Question

For review.

At the end of every two years, the NTCIP Leadership Role must rotate such that the Chair must transition to the Junior Vice-Chair, the Junior Vice-Chair will transition to the Senior Vice-Chair, and the Senior Vice-Chair will transition to the Chair. If an SDO has left their seat empty for more than 6 months at the time of rotation, their position will be assigned to the Junior Vice Chair. If this condition applies to multiple SDOs, the Junior Vice Chair will be assigned to the SDO with the longest consecutive period of vacancy. These rules can be varied by the joint decision of the SDOs.

The assignee to a NTCIP Leadership Role can resign or be replaced by their appointing SDO at any time.

NTCIP JC Members¶

Parameter Value
Effective Date: September 30, 2026
Last Reviewed/Updated: September 30, 2026

Assignment¶

Voting members of the JC are appointed by the SDOs with six members appointed by each of the three SDOs, for a total of 18 voting member positions. Membership within the NTCIP JC is assigned to an individual, but that individual may designate a single alternate member from the same organization to represent the assigned member's position if the assigned member is unavailable. It is the responsibility of the SDOs to coordinate among each other to ensure balance among these members, including that no single organization is represented by more than a single voting membership.

SDOs are encouraged to identify terms for each of their appointed NTCIP JC members so that all parties can better manage expectations. The SDO can reassign a member to a new term at the end of the current term.

SDOs may apply additional requirements to their appointed NTCIP JC members, such as requiring that the member be employed in the public or private sector. Enforcement of any additional requirements is the responsibility of the SDO.

Participation¶

NTCIP JC members are responsible for participating in meetings. SDOs are responsible for ensuring that their appointed NTCIP JC members participate in meetings and removing them otherwise. It is recommended that SDOs warn NTCIP JC members that their membership status is in jeopardy if they miss two consecutive meetings and to remove them if they miss three consecutive meetings.

Note

NTCIP JC members are appointed by the SDOs. It is the responsibility of the SDOs to ensure that their appointed NTCIP JC members participate in meetings and remove them otherwise. Failure of an SDO to ensure member participation results in a defacto dilution of the SDO vote.

Responsibilities¶

In addition to participating in meetings, NTCIP JC members are responsible for:

  • Participating in discussions within the JC,
  • Reviewing documents,
  • Providing comments,
  • Casting votes, and
  • Informing the NTCIP Coordinator of any changes to their contact or employment information.

Alternates¶

Discussion

This is a significant change from the draft MOU; for review.

A voting member of the NTCIP JC may designate an alternate member to represent and vote for the voting member if the assigned voting member is unavailable for a meeting. The alternate member must not be employed from an organization already represented by another voting member or alternate in attendance and must meet any additional requirements imposed by the respective SDO (e.g., An SDO could require that the alternate member work in the public or private sector).

A voting member must not be allowed to rely upon an alternate member at two consecutive meetings.

Note

While the SDOs are allowed to establish their own alternate member policies, the goal is for SDOs to appoint voting members who will personally attend most meetings of the NTCIP JC. The alternate member policy is to allow for exceptional cases and reliance on alternate members at consecutive meetings is discouraged.

Example

Meeting 1: Voting member is present.
Meeting 2: Voting member assigns alternate.
Meeting 3: Voting member is present.
Meeting 4: Voting member assigns alternate.

Meeting 1: Voting member is present.
Meeting 2: Voting member assigns alternate.
Meeting 3: Voting member is absent; alternate not allowed. (SDO should warn the voting member that their voting membership is in jeopardy)
Meeting 4: Voting member is present.

Meeting 1: Voting member is present.
Meeting 2: Voting member absent; alternate not assigned.
Meeting 3: Voting member assigns alternate. (SDO should warn the voting member that their voting membership is in jeopardy)
Meeting 4: Voting member is present.

Meeting 1: Voting member is present.
Meeting 2: Voting member assigns alternate.
Meeting 3: Voting member is absent; alternate not allowed. (SDO should warn the voting member that their voting membership is in jeopardy)
Meeting 4: Voting member assigns alternate.
SDO is encouraged to remove the voting member at the end of Meeting 4

Termination¶

A member of the NTCIP JC can resign or be replaced by their appointing SDO at any time. SDOs are encouraged to remove a voting member who frequently misses meetings.

Group Chairs¶

Parameter Value
Effective Date: September 30, 2026
Last Reviewed/Updated: September 30, 2026

Assignment¶

The NTCIP JC Chair must appoint a chair and a vice-chair for each new NTCIP group established by the NTCIP JC and must fill any vacancies in existing groups.

Chairs and vice-chairs are typically voting members of the group that they lead, but are not required to be voting members.

Example

A chair who is a voting member can change their employment and as a consequence change their interest category. If there is no empty voting position for the new interest category, the chair can transition to a non-voting observer while retaining the chair role.

Question

Should there be a defined term? Should the Vice-Chair become the Chair at the end of the term?

Responsibilities¶

The Chair and Vice-Chair are responsible for:

  1. Acting on behalf of the full NTCIP effort (not their employer)
  2. Reporting to the NTCIP JC
  3. Ensuring projects are moving forwards
  4. Calling group meetings
  5. Presiding over group meetings
    • Ensuring that important topics are included in the agenda
    • Pursuing goal of establishing consensus on important issues
    • Ensuring that all concerns are properly considered by the group in a balanced manner
    • Ensuring that all group decisions are properly formulated and captured
  6. Ensuring established processes are followed
  7. Ensuring the development of a long-term plan for the group to be recommended to the JC

In all cases, the Chair takes the lead in these activities. The Vice-Chair assists the Chair in these activities and acts as the Chair in the absence of the Chair.

Termination¶

A chair or vice-chair of an NTCIP group can resign at any time. The NTCIP JC Chair can remove the chair or vice-chair from their role at any time.

Question

Technically, this would allow a new NTCIP JC Chair to clean house and remove all current group chairs and vice-chairs. Is this appropriate? Maybe we say that the chair or vice-chair can be removed from their role at any time by simple majority vote among the NTCIP JC Chair, Senior Vice-Chair, and Junior Vice-Chair.

Member Assignment¶

For JC Discussion

Voting membership on the WGs has become an issue. They are incompatible with the proposed IFR approval process because ANSI requires safety-related standards to have no more than ⅓ of the vote reserved for any interest category. We currently only divide between public and private sector - at best achieving 50% balance. In addition, concerns have been raised about the opaque nature in which members are appointed and there have been significant problems in achieving quorum at times, which delays the work. The following represents the consensus of the WG but needs review by the JC.

Membership within each group is assigned to an individual.

Voting Membership¶

The number of voting positions within a group are established by the NTCIP JC.

Question

The previous rules had the co-chairs proposing voting members and the JC chair approving. The following is what I think we agreed to in discussions. Please review and let me know if we should revise.

The initial assignment of voting members to fill these positions must be performed jointly by the NTCIP JC chair and the group's co-chairs. Subsequent assignments of vacancies are performed by the group's co-chairs. In all cases, the assignments must:

  • Ensure the members fulfill the requirements for the position, including that the proposed member:
    • Represents the interest category associated with the voting position (e.g., only users can be assigned to a user position)
    • Does not represent the same organization as another voting member
    • Does not have a conflict of interest
  • Prefer members who:
    • Have indicated a willingness to serve
    • Have demonstrated a record of meeting attendance
    • Represent significant perspectives of the ITS industry not otherwise represented in the voting membership (e.g., if this limitation was not otherwise listed, the perspectives of a single project could be over-represented by appointing representative from its associated state agencies, local agencies, consultants, manufacturers, etc.)
    • Who are not being directly or indirectly funded by an entity, other than their employer, to perform a key role (e.g., editor) for the group.

Example

It is preferred to avoid assigning voting membership to a consultant who has been hired to be the editor of the document that the group is developing. Consultants who are not being funded as editor for the current document can be assigned as a general interest voting member without any bias. If a chair has difficulty in filling a voting position for the interest category of the editor, the chair can assign the editor to that empty position.

Question

We talked about having terms; here is my proposed text. Please review.

A normal term for a voting member is three (3) years. The chair may establish a shorter term for a voting member at the start of a new term. Voting members may be reappointed to additional terms; there are not any term limits.

Example

The chair can assign a shorter term in order to ensure staggered terms among voting members.

Liaison Membership¶

Liaisons from other organizations must be recognized when appointed by other organizations.

Observer Membership¶

Any entity can participate at any meeting as an observer.

Responsibilities¶

In addition to participating in meetings, NTCIP group members are responsible for:

  • Participating in discussions within the group,
  • Reviewing documents,
  • Providing comments,
  • Informing the NTCIP Coordinator of any changes to their contact or employment information.

In addition, voting members are responsible for:

  • Casting votes and
  • Appointing their alternate.

Group Alternates¶

A voting member of an NTCIP group may designate an alternate member to represent and vote for the voting member if the assigned voting member is unavailable for a meeting. The alternate member must:

  1. Represent the same interest category as the voting member
  2. Not be employed or represent the same organization as another voting or active alternate member (an alternate member may be employed or represent the same organization as the voting member that is being replaced)
  3. Not have a conflict of interest
  4. Count towards quorum and voting results (when not abstaining)

Alternate members that are not employed by the same organization as the voting member must be designated in advance of the meeting; self-declared alternates at the meeting are only allowed when employed by the same organization as the voting member.

Attendance by an alternate member does not count towards the voting member's attendance.

Terminating Voting Membership¶

Question

For review

Voting members of a group are responsible for participating in the group's meetings. Any voting member that misses three out of four consecutive meetings automatically transition to observer status, unless the member appeals to the chair and the chair approves an exception that justifies the exception and specifies the conditions that will be required for the member to regain voting membership.

Note

The chair is provided with some discretion in applying the attendance rule to allow for exceptional cases. However, as an prolonged empty seat affects balance of interest categories, the chair should only approve exceptions in rare cases and only after careful consideration.

Note

A warning from the NTCIP Coordinator is a courtesy and does not absolve the voting member from the responsibility to attend meetings. The voting membership lapses even if the NTCIP Coordinator fails to issue the warning.

Note

Attendance, or lack of attendance, at a properly publicized meeting that is immediately cancelled due to lack of quorum counts towards the voting member's attendance.

Note

The change in status occurs at the end of the meeting; allowing a member to enter a meeting late without penalty.

Voting members may resign at any time. Groups may remove a voting member by a ⅔rds vote.

Each voting member of a group represents an interest category. If a voting member changes their employment and as a consequence changes their interest category, the voting member must transition to observer status. If there is an empty position for the new interest category, the chair may assign the person to the position in the new interest category.

Terminating Group Membership¶

A member of an NTCIP group can resign at any time. An observing member who misses five consecutive meetings may be removed from the group's membership role (but can be re-instated by simply attending a meeting).

Note

Removing members from roles is primarily a task to ensure that a group is still viable and that membership lists reflect the current membership.

Editors¶

Parameter Value
Effective Date: September 30, 2026
Last Reviewed/Updated: September 30, 2026

Assignment¶

Every NTCIP document (draft or published) is assigned a primary editor by the NTCIP Coordinator with input from the NTCIP JC chair and the relevant group's co-chairs.

Responsibilities¶

The editor is responsible for consolidating all of the comments received to date into a coherent format, such as the one defined in NTCIP 8002 or within the GitHub repository. The editor is also responsible for the following, per the processes and procedures defined in this document:

  1. Preparing a draft of the document per WG input
  2. Managing written comments received
  3. Formulating draft responses
  4. Facilitating discussions on the comments and draft responses
  5. Submitting the final comments with resolutions to the NTCIP Coordinator
  6. Developing any required standards development report package

Termination¶

The editor can resign at any time. The NTCIP Coordinator can remove the editor from their role at any time.

Assignment¶

For online development projects, the NTCIP Coordinator must assign a maintainer. The maintainer can be the editor.

Responsibilities¶

The maintainer is responsible for the tasks defined for this role in NTCIP 8008. The maintainer is responsible for the management of the online environment and does not need to necessarily be a subject matter expert in relation to the standard; rather the maintainer must be familiar with GitHub operations to ensure that the online environment remains useful, that those submitting comments receive timely acknowledgement, and must coordinate with the editor to ensure subject matter issues are resolved.

Termination¶

The maintainer can resign at any time. The NTCIP Coordinator can remove the maintainer from their role at any time.

Liaison¶

Parameter Value
Effective Date: September 30, 2026
Last Reviewed/Updated: September 30, 2026

Liaisons are members of a group that have been formally designated to be responsible for reporting to and/or reporting from an external organization that is relevant to the group's discussion. The formal designation often facilitates sharing information between groups. Liaisons can be voting members or observers but have no additional rights at meetings.

Assignment¶

The group chair must recognize liaisons appointed by other groups that have established a formal relationship with the NTCIP effort.

The group chair in cooperation with the NTCIP coordinator and NTCIP JC chair may assign any group member to be a liaison to an external organization. The NTCIP coordinator must support this activity by entering into any appropriate and necessary agreements with the external organization and maintaining a list of liaisons of the NTCIP effort. Liaisons can be voting members or observers but have no additional rights at meetings.

Responsibilities¶

Liaisons are responsible for sharing information and documents between the NTCIP effort and the external organization. Importantly, they should update the NTCIP group about any significant developments or decisions made by the external organization.

Termination¶

The liaison can resign at any time. The NTCIP group Chair and NTCIP JC CHair can remove an outgoing liaison from their role at any time.

NTCIP JC meetings are open to any party with a vested interest in ITS. Interested parties should contact the NTCIP coordinator in advance of attending a meeting to be added to the list of observers. NTCIP Development Process¶

Parameter Value
Effective Date: September 30, 2017
Last Reviewed/Updated: September 30, 2026

Process Statement¶

Overview¶

In concept, the process of creating a standard is straightforward: a specification undergoes a period of development and several iterations of review and revision, is approved (or adopted) as a standard by the appropriate body, and is published. In practice, the process is more complicated, due to:

  1. the difficulty of creating specifications of high technical quality;
  2. the need to consider the interests of all of the affected parties;
  3. the importance of establishing widespread community consensus; and
  4. the difficulty of evaluating the utility of a particular specification.

Goals¶

The goals of the NTCIP Standards Process are:

  1. technical excellence;
  2. clear, concise, and easily understood documentation;
  3. openness and fairness; and
  4. meeting needs within the ITS community.

The goals of technical excellence and the need to allow all interested parties to comment on work items require significant time and effort. On the other hand, today's rapid development of technology demands the timely development of standards. The NTCIP standards process is intended to balance these conflicting goals. The process is believed to be as short and simple as possible without unduly sacrificing technical excellence or openness and fairness.

From its inception, the NTCIP project has been, and is expected to remain, an evolving family of standards whose members regularly factor new requirements and technology into its design and implementation. Users of NTCIP standards and providers of the equipment, software, and services that support it should anticipate and embrace this evolution as a major tenet of NTCIP philosophy.

Document Approval Processes¶

To understand the NTCIP development and approval processes, it is important to distinguish among three orthogonal concepts: - The intended type of NTCIP document to be produced, such as a technical specification, an informational report, etc. - The development process that define the mechanics of producing draft documents. For example, NTCIP 8002 defines how MS Word can be used whereas NTCIP 8008 defines a more agile approach that uses GitHub. - The NTCIP standardization levels, which are associated with approval procedures.

All types of NTCIP documents (e.g., technical specifications, informational reports) intended for publication can use either of the development processes and target any of the standardization levels. Likewise, either development processes can be used to produce documents at any of the standardization levels.

The NTCIP has three standardization levels. The Interim Document for Field Release (IFR) has its own approval procedure while the Recommended Standard (RS) is an interim step of the full standardization procedure.
